🎯 Project Overview

This prosthetic hand was developed to address the significant cost barrier in assistive technology. Traditional prosthetics can cost tens of thousands of dollars, making them inaccessible to many who need them. By leveraging modern 3D printing technology and innovative mechanical design, this project aims to create a functional alternative that can be produced for under $500.

The design features a cable-driven finger mechanism that provides natural grip patterns and sufficient force for everyday tasks. Every component was optimized for 3D printing, ensuring that the device can be manufactured with commonly available equipment.

🔧 Design Process

Research & Requirements
Conducted user research and literature review to understand key functional requirements, grip patterns, and design constraints for upper-limb prosthetics.
Initial CAD Modeling
Created first iteration in Onshape, focusing on basic finger articulation and grip mechanics. Designed around a cable-driven system for simplicity and reliability.
Prototype & Testing
3D printed prototype components and assembled for functional testing. Identified issues with joint strength and cable routing that required design revision.
Iteration & Refinement
Redesigned finger joints with reinforced geometry, improved cable guides, and better mechanical advantage for grip strength. Added ergonomic palm shape.
Final Validation
Conducted grip strength tests and durability assessments. Validated that the design could handle everyday objects and maintain functionality over extended use.

💡 What I Learned

This project pushed me to think deeply about design constraints and manufacturing limitations. Working with 3D printing meant every feature had to be printable without support structures, which forced creative problem-solving in joint design and assembly.

I gained hands-on experience with iterative design, learning that the first version is rarely the right version. Each test revealed new insights about material properties, mechanical advantage, and user needs. The process taught me patience and the value of documentation.

Most importantly, this project reinforced why engineering matters. Designing something that could genuinely improve someone's quality of life added a sense of purpose beyond technical achievement.

Key Technical Skills Developed:

  • Advanced CAD modeling in Onshape with parametric design
  • Design for additive manufacturing (DfAM) principles
  • Mechanical systems design including cables, pulleys, and linkages
  • Material selection for functional 3D printed parts
  • Assembly planning and fastener specification
  • Testing protocols for mechanical devices
