About Me

What drives me, who I aspire to be, and what I want you to know

👤

Hello, I'm Reid Ellis

I'm a Computer Science and Cybersecurity Major driven by curiosity and a passion for creating things. Whether I'm writing code, designing prosthetics, or building competition robots, I approach every challenge with the same goal: Learn something new, create something novel.

What matters most to me is that I am always learning new things, and making an impact on the world. I believe that the best projects comes from understanding not just how things work, but why they matter and who they serve.

My journey spans from engineering projects like competitive robotics and Science Olympiad to computer science projects like machine learning and web development. Each project teaches me something new about problem-solving, collaboration, and pushing beyond my comfort zone.

What I Value

🔄 Continuous Growth

My dream is to learn as a career, and as a hobby. Stepping into the real world and constantly expanding my knowledge, while working with people who are learning along side me, sounds like the perfect life.

🤝 Collaboration & Community

The best solutions come from diverse perspectives. My greatest learning experiences come from projects like hackathons, FIRST robotics, and project based classes where I was learning alongside my friends and mentors.

⚡ Innovation Through Action

To innovate is to learn. Learing new ways of solving problems, discovering problems we didn't even know existed yet, and then solving them. The best way to learn and innovate is through the creation of projects that challenge you and lead to the development of new projects.

Beyond the Code

I enjoy creating things, whether it is debugging an application for a hackathon, or spending hours in Blender and OnShape to create prosthetic molds, it is incredibly satisfying to work on something to learn something new.

I believe in building things that last and matter. That means writing clean, maintainable code. Designing with the end user in mind. Testing thoroughly. Documenting clearly. The details matter because they're what separate a finished project from an abandoned experiment. I believe in a sustainable problem solving community.

Outside of my technical work, I'm driven by the same curiosity that fuels my projects. I'm always exploring new technologies, reading about emerging fields, and looking for connections between seemingly unrelated disciplines. Some of my best insights have come from unexpected places.
